This dataset documents 56 extradition or surrender partners for France. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 6 bilateral treaty partners; 21 multilateral convention partners; 29 surrender mechanism partners. Categories overlap. Of 56 documented partner jurisdictions, 56 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Research is partial. No complete bilateral and multilateral treaty inventory is certified. Unknown relationships are not findings that extradition is unavailable.

Recorded legal frameworks

Council Framework Decision 2002/584/JHA of 13 June 2002 on the European arrest warrant and the surrender procedures between Member States (26 recorded partners); European Convention on Extradition (ETS No. 024) (21 recorded partners); Agreement between the European Union and the Republic of Iceland and the Kingdom of Norway on the surrender procedure between the Member States of the European Union and Iceland and Norway (2 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

French law restricts ordinary extradition of French nationals. European Arrest Warrant surrender follows different rules and can include French nationals. Research status: incomplete; see source-access qualifications below.

Rules, exceptions and sources

Code of Criminal Procedure Article 696-4(1) bars ordinary extradition where the person was French at the time of the alleged offence. The applicable treaty framework must also be considered.

Under Article 695-32, European Arrest Warrant surrender can be conditioned on return to France to serve a resulting sentence for French nationals and qualifying residents. Article 695-24(2) provides a possible refusal ground for sentence-enforcement requests where the sentence can be enforced in France.

The ordinary extradition nationality rule is not a blanket rule for European Arrest Warrants or other separate surrender procedures.

The Légifrance provisions and the Court of Cassation's 11 March 2025 explanation were available through official indexed extracts. Direct automated retrieval was blocked by access controls. This profile remains incomplete pending direct-source review and treaty-specific qualifications.; Légifrance marks Article 695-32 for repeal from 1 January 2029, after this snapshot; it remains relevant on 22 October 2026.
