This dataset documents 53 extradition or surrender partners for Monaco. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 3 bilateral treaty partners; 50 multilateral convention partners. Categories overlap. Of 53 documented partner jurisdictions, 53 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Research is partial. No complete bilateral and multilateral treaty inventory is certified. Unknown relationships are not findings that extradition is unavailable.

Recorded legal frameworks

European Convention on Extradition (ETS No. 024) (50 recorded partners); Treaty on extradition between Australia and the Government of his Serene Highness the Prince of Monaco (1 recorded partners); Treaty between Great Britain and Monaco for the Extradition of Criminals (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

Monaco's extradition law states that it does not extradite its nationals. A refused request can instead be referred for possible prosecution in Monaco.

Rules, exceptions and sources

Article 7 of Law No. 1.222 states the nationality restriction. If extradition is refused for that reason, the requesting state may ask for the case to be sent to the Prosecutor General for prosecution where appropriate.

Article 1 establishes the role of applicable treaties and the statutory rules where treaties are absent or silent.

This rule does not establish immunity from prosecution in Monaco or govern how another country treats a Monegasque citizen found there.

Article 7's current wording took effect on 1 January 2024.
