This dataset documents 77 extradition or surrender partners for United Kingdom. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 28 bilateral treaty partners; 23 multilateral convention partners; 27 surrender mechanism partners. Categories overlap. Of 77 documented partner jurisdictions, 76 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Research is partial. No complete bilateral and multilateral treaty inventory is certified. Unknown relationships are not findings that extradition is unavailable.

Recorded legal frameworks

Trade and Cooperation Agreement between the European Union and the European Atomic Energy Community, of the one part, and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, of the other part — Part Three, Title VII (Surrender) (27 recorded partners); European Convention on Extradition (ETS No. 024) (23 recorded partners); Agreement between the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland and the Republic of Albania Supplementing the European Convention on Extradition 1957 (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

British nationality does not itself prevent extradition from the United Kingdom. The applicable statutory safeguards still have to be met.

Rules, exceptions and sources

The United Kingdom's official national-procedure statement confirms no bar on extraditing UK nationals. Current Crown Prosecution Service guidance also states that the UK has not asserted a nationality bar under the UK–EU surrender arrangements.

The Extradition Act 2003 provides the domestic process. Statutory bars, human-rights requirements and the particular request remain relevant regardless of citizenship.

For the UK–US treaty specifically, Article 3 prevents refusal based on the requested person's nationality.

A foreign country's own nationality bar may affect a request made by the UK. It is not a British citizenship exemption from extradition out of the UK.

The Council of Europe country statement is dated 19 September 2018; its nationality statement was cross-checked against current CPS guidance and the official treaty explanation.
