This dataset documents 116 extradition or surrender partners for United States. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 113 bilateral treaty partners; 11 multilateral convention partners; 3 other arrangement partners. Categories overlap. Of 116 documented partner jurisdictions, 115 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

US Code treaty notes include historic entries. Known obsolete entries have been corrected or excluded, but all supplements and successor-state issues have not been comprehensively audited. Consult the cited instrument and current government advice.

Recorded legal frameworks

Convention on Extradition (Montevideo, 1933) (11 recorded partners); Extradition Treaty between the United States of America and the Argentine Republic (1 recorded partners); Treaty on extradition between Australia and the United States of America (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

US citizens can be extradited from the United States under an applicable treaty or convention. Citizenship alone is not a general exemption. Research status: incomplete; see source-access qualifications below.

Rules, exceptions and sources

18 USC 3196 allows the Secretary of State to surrender a US citizen even when the applicable treaty does not require extradition of nationals, provided the treaty or convention's other requirements are met.

This provision is tied to an applicable treaty or convention. It does not by itself create unrestricted power to extradite a citizen to every country.

The treaty, judicial extradition process and executive decision must be considered for the particular request.

No individual treaty's nationality clause is inferred from this general statutory power.; Current OLRC indexed text corroborates the 2024 statutory wording, but direct access to the current consolidation failed; a complete amendment audit remains outstanding.
