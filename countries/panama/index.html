This dataset documents 21 extradition or surrender partners for Panama. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 5 bilateral treaty partners; 17 multilateral convention partners. Categories overlap. Of 21 documented partner jurisdictions, 21 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Research is partial. No complete bilateral and multilateral treaty inventory is certified. Unknown relationships are not findings that extradition is unavailable.

Recorded legal frameworks

Convention on Extradition (Montevideo, 1933) (13 recorded partners); Inter-American Convention on Extradition (5 recorded partners); Treaty between the United Kingdom and Panama for the Mutual Surrender of Fugitive Criminals (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Convention on Extradition (Montevideo, 1933)

Extradition convention among deposited parties, subject to reservations and offence-date limits. Article 23 permits accession by non-signatory states without a separate partner-acceptance condition. Article 21 preserves earlier treaties; this convention does not override them. The United States says it does not in practice extradite solely under a multilateral convention without a bilateral treaty; US–Kazakhstan and US–Ukraine are withheld pending applicability review.

The OAS depositary lists 26 December 1934 as general entry into force. Article 20 provides entry into force thirty days after deposit for each state; exact member effective dates have not been independently reconciled.
