This dataset documents 44 extradition or surrender partners for China. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 44 bilateral treaty partners. Categories overlap. Of 44 documented partner jurisdictions, 44 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Official Chinese titles and recorded entry-into-force dates are retained. The China–Hungary treaty is recorded as signed, with entry into force unverified; it does not turn Hungary blue.

Recorded legal frameworks

中华人民共和国和阿富汗伊斯兰共和国引渡条约 (1 recorded partners); 中华人民共和国和阿尔及利亚民主人民共和国引渡条约 (1 recorded partners); 中华人民共和国和安哥拉共和国引渡条约 (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

China's Extradition Law requires refusal when the requested person is a Chinese national under Chinese law.

Rules, exceptions and sources

Article 8(1) of the Extradition Law makes Chinese nationality a mandatory refusal ground for a foreign state's extradition request to the People's Republic of China.

The rule refers to nationality as determined by Chinese law. Holding another passport does not itself establish how Chinese nationality law will treat the person.

This rule does not establish immunity from investigation or prosecution in China.

UNODC's reproduction of the legislation and an official Chinese government reproduction were checked. Separate nationality-law questions and special territorial systems have not been comprehensively researched.
