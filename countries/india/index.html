This dataset documents 60 extradition or surrender partners for India. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 48 bilateral treaty partners; 12 other arrangement partners. Categories overlap. Of 60 documented partner jurisdictions, 59 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

India’s official list distinguishes treaties and other arrangements. The Italy and Croatia arrangements are limited to narcotics offences. Some official titles and exact dates remain untranscribed.

Recorded legal frameworks

Extradition Treaty between the Republic of India and the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan (1 recorded partners); Extradition treaty between Australia and the Republic of India (1 recorded partners); Extradition Treaty between the Republic of India and the Republic of Azerbaijan (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

Indian nationality is not a general statutory bar to extradition from India. The particular treaty can impose a different rule.

Rules, exceptions and sources

India's Ministry of External Affairs states that the Extradition Act 1962 does not prohibit extradition of Indian nationals, but the relevant treaty controls the nationality question.

Treaty wording varies: some clauses prohibit extradition of nationals, some allow refusal, and others permit surrender at the requested state's discretion. These categories should not be treated as equivalent.

A treaty-by-treaty nationality clause must be checked; the Ministry's illustrative table is not a complete current audit of every treaty.

The Ministry's explanatory page has no clear provision-by-provision currency date. This profile does not determine an individual's citizenship, including issues involving Overseas Citizen of India status.

  • Indian Ministry of External Affairs: extradition from India — extradition of own nationals
  • India–Republic of Korea Treaty on Extradition, Article 6 (example of discretionary nationality clause)
