This dataset documents 55 extradition or surrender partners for Switzerland. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 5 bilateral treaty partners; 50 multilateral convention partners. Categories overlap. Of 55 documented partner jurisdictions, 55 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Research is partial. No complete bilateral and multilateral treaty inventory is certified. Unknown relationships are not findings that extradition is unavailable.

Recorded legal frameworks

European Convention on Extradition (ETS No. 024) (50 recorded partners); Treaty between Australia and Switzerland on extradition (1 recorded partners); Tratado de Extradição entre o Brasil e a Suíça (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

Swiss citizens may be extradited from Switzerland to a foreign authority only with their consent under the federal constitution.

Rules, exceptions and sources

Federal Constitution Article 25(1) requires a Swiss citizen's consent for extradition to a foreign authority and also prohibits expulsion of Swiss citizens from Switzerland.

This nationality safeguard does not itself resolve domestic criminal proceedings or the conditions of any particular extradition request.

Special international-court surrender procedures and the legal requirements for valid consent are outside this introductory profile.

The official English constitutional translation is informative; the authoritative German, French and Italian texts govern.

  • Fedlex: Swiss Federal Constitution, Article 25
  • Swiss Parliament: Federal Constitution, Article 25 (2016 English compilation)

Last verification recorded: 22 October 2026. These rules concern requests made to Switzerland, not protection abroad.
