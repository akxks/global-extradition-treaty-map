This dataset documents 35 extradition or surrender partners for Brazil. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 29 bilateral treaty partners; 6 multilateral convention partners. Categories overlap. Of 35 documented partner jurisdictions, 35 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Brazil’s official bilateral inventory is dated September 2026. CPLP relationships use verified ratifications, not organisation membership; the Brazil–Portugal bilateral basis is superseded.

Recorded legal frameworks

Convenção de Extradição entre os Estados Membros da Comunidade dos Países de Língua Portuguesa (6 recorded partners); Tratado de Extradição com a Argentina (1 recorded partners); Treaty on extradition between Australia and the Federative Republic of Brazil (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

Brazil's constitution restricts extradition of Brazilian nationals, with specified exceptions for naturalised citizens.

Rules, exceptions and sources

Article 5(LI) prohibits extradition of Brazilians except naturalised citizens for ordinary crimes committed before naturalisation or proven involvement in illicit trafficking in narcotic drugs and related substances, as provided by law.

The constitutional distinction concerns citizenship by birth and by naturalisation. A person's legal status and the timing and nature of the alleged conduct matter.

The naturalisation and drug-trafficking exceptions require legal assessment. The rule does not prevent domestic criminal proceedings.

This is an extradition summary, not an analysis of surrender to international courts or every citizenship-loss and dual-nationality question.; Official indexed text was available; direct automated retrieval of the main source was intermittently unavailable.
