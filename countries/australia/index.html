This dataset documents 53 extradition or surrender partners for Australia. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 52 bilateral treaty partners; 1 surrender mechanism partners. Categories overlap. Of 53 documented partner jurisdictions, 53 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Bilateral treaties, specifically listed Commonwealth arrangements and New Zealand surrender are distinguished. One-way domestic regulation records appear only in relationship details. Australian source lists date from 2019 or 2024; coverage is not certified complete through October 2026.

Recorded legal frameworks

Extradition Treaty between Great Britain and the Albanian Republic (1 recorded partners); Treaty on extradition between the Government of Australia and the Government of the Republic of Argentina (1 recorded partners); Treaty between Australia and the Republic of Austria concerning extradition (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

Australian citizens can be extradited from Australia. The applicable treaty may allow refusal on nationality grounds, and the other legal safeguards still apply.

Rules, exceptions and sources

The Extradition Act 1988 provides the statutory framework, including extradition objections and the Attorney-General's surrender decision. Australian government reports document surrender of Australian nationals under that framework.

Nationality clauses vary by treaty. For example, the Australia–Monaco convention permits Australia to decline surrender of its nationals, while Monaco's nationality clause is mandatory. A discretionary clause does not guarantee refusal.

The Act, relevant regulations and treaty conditions must all be checked. New Zealand requests use a distinct backing-of-warrants procedure.

The Act compilation dated 27 August 2026 was checked. The government report illustrates historical surrender of nationals; it is not a prediction of a current case's outcome.
