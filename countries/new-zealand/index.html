This dataset documents 5 extradition or surrender partners for New Zealand. These agreements provide legal frameworks for requesting a person's surrender, subject to the applicable laws and restrictions. The inventory is incomplete; an unlisted country is not evidence of no treaty.

The recorded network includes 3 bilateral treaty partners; 1 surrender mechanism partners; 1 other arrangement partners. Categories overlap. Of 5 documented partner jurisdictions, 5 are UN member or observer states used in the network rankings.

Verified treaty and backing-of-warrants routes are distinguished. The Hong Kong agreement is suspended; historical inherited treaty coverage remains incomplete. Sources disagree on some dates, which are flagged in the relevant records.

Recorded legal frameworks

Agreement on Extradition between the Government of New Zealand and the Government of the Republic of Fiji (1 recorded partners); Treaty on Extradition between New Zealand and the Republic of Korea (1 recorded partners).

These are evidenced legal bases, not assurances of extradition. Conditions, reservations and domestic law must be checked for the particular request.

Citizenship and nationality

New Zealand generally applies its extradition law to citizens and non-citizens alike. Treaty provisions and other legal conditions can create exceptions.

Rules, exceptions and sources

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade states that citizenship generally does not distinguish requests under the Extradition Act 1999. It identifies possible exceptions in treaties, Orders in Council, undertakings and arrangements.

The Ministry describes separate backed-warrant procedures for requests from Australia and the United Kingdom. A nationality summary alone does not determine the applicable procedure.

Check the relevant treaty, statutory procedure and any undertaking; there is no general passport-based guarantee of refusal.

Official guidance was checked for the nationality statement. Its historical treaty list is not used here as proof that every listed treaty remains operational; the HK treaty's suspension requires separate treatment.
