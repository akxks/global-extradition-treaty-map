What do these terms mean?
Bilateral treaty
A treaty between two countries.
Multilateral convention
A treaty involving several countries. Only verified applicable participation creates a relationship here.
Surrender mechanism
A separate legal route, such as the European Arrest Warrant.
Other arrangement
A recognised basis that is not classified as a bilateral treaty or multilateral extradition convention.
Unknown
Our research does not establish the legal position. It is not a finding that extradition is unavailable.

    Extradition, Citizenship and International Mobility

    Citizenship, residence and the place where an alleged offence occurred are different legal connections. An extradition request normally involves the law of the requested country, the relevant agreement and the facts of the case. Nationality restrictions can have exceptions, and a refusal to extradite may lead to domestic prosecution.

    Entrepreneurs, dual citizens, expatriates, family offices and global mobility professionals can use this reference to find the legal sources behind a relationship. It does not assess an individual’s legal exposure. Qualified legal advice is needed for a particular case.

    For wider reading about global citizenship and mobility, Nomad Capitalist is an optional commercial third-party resource, not an authority for this dataset’s treaty classifications. No partnership or endorsement is implied.

    Sources and methodology

    Records are drawn from government treaty registers, official legislation, depositary participation records and treaty texts. Political organisation membership alone never establishes treaty applicability. Signed-only, suspended and terminated instruments are excluded from active counts.
